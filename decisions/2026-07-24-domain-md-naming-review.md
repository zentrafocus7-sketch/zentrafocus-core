**Title:** Architectural review of DOMAIN.md naming vs. README.md / INDEX.md — confirmed as-is
**Type:** Architectural
**Decision:** A concern was raised that `DOMAIN.md` becomes an increasingly generic filename as a domain accumulates policy documents (e.g., `brand/IDENTITY.md`, `VOICE.md`, `VISUAL_SYSTEM.md`), and that `README.md` or `INDEX.md` would be more self-explanatory. Reviewed against Simplicity, Single Point of Truth, long-term maintainability, human readability, and AI readability. Conclusion: `DOMAIN.md` is retained, unchanged.

Reasoning: (1) Simplicity — no current requirement was named that `DOMAIN.md` fails to meet; the concern is about a hypothetical future folder state, not a present defect, which Principle 8 does not treat as sufficient grounds to change a working design. (2) Single Point of Truth — root `README.md` is already defined by Blueprint as the repository's one narrative onboarding entry point; `DOMAIN.md` is a structured eight-field record, matching the shape of `DECISION_TEMPLATE.md`/`POLICY_TEMPLATE.md`, not a narrative document. Naming both "README.md" would make one filename mean two structurally different things depending on folder, which Principle 2 treats as a defect. `INDEX.md` avoids the collision but supplies no offsetting benefit and isn't used as a convention anywhere else in Core. (3) Long-term maintainability — `DOMAIN.md` was a deliberate choice made in Task 08 specifically to keep domains minimal; reopening it requires new evidence per Principle 12, not a restated preference. (4) Human and AI readability — as a domain fills with policy documents (all named for the rule they govern), `DOMAIN.md` is deliberately the one file that is *not* a policy document; its distinct name is what lets a reader or agent tell the domain's own record apart from its policies at a glance. A generic name would erase that distinction at the exact moment it matters most. (5) GitHub's auto-rendering of README.md in folder views was considered and rejected as a rationale — it is a property of one hosting platform, and Governance explicitly requires Core's structure to remain valid independent of which tools are in use.

No files were renamed. No cross-references were changed. No templates were changed.
